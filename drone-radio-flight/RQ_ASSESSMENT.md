# When does sending a missed scan help a drone team?
Research assessment, 20 November 2026. Scope: simulated cooperative first detection of a single drifting target. This is a targeted review of relevant 2026 publications, not a systematic novelty search.

## Recommended primary question

Under a common hard communication budget, when does a negative scan have enough mission value to share, and can a decentralized RL sender trained on some channel and detector conditions reduce capped time to first detection on unseen conditions relative to silence and the best validation-selected simple sender?

## Published gaps and boundaries on originality

1. Yuan et al., Applied Sciences, June 2026, section 4.8 and concluding limitations: cooperative UAV MAPPO with limited communication already exists. Their robustness experiments retrained each configuration. They explicitly propose zero-shot transfer and testing unseen detection-noise levels. https://doi.org/10.3390/app16126086
2. Niu, Yi and Hong, Sensors, August 2026, conclusions: explicitly propose moving targets and testing asynchronous execution, delays, packet loss and intermittent connectivity. Their system shares positive events for multiple stationary targets. Our single-target search ends upon detection, making the evidence exchanged before detection unsuccessful scans. https://doi.org/10.3390/s26165189
3. Zhao et al., Sensors, 2026: fixed-rate, staggered communication with a frozen exploration policy already improves performance in their setting. They find little benefit on planners that barely use shared beliefs. This motivates a matched-rate staggered comparator and a decision-sensitivity diagnostic. https://doi.org/10.3390/s26154778
4. Wang et al., Ad Hoc Networks, September 2026: maritime search already combines DRL with communication gating and adaptive altitude. We verified this overlap from the publisher abstract and highlights. We do not attribute a retry gap to these authors. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.adhoc.2026.104277
5. Heterogeneous agents cyclic path coverage and multi-target pursuit in maritime search and rescue missions, available online October 26, 2026: irregularly drifting targets and updated grid uncertainty already appear in maritime search. Its heterogeneous UAV/USV allocation and pursuit objective differs from first detection. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1000936126004814

## What survived review

The earlier 280-run PyBullet comparison found 265 detections with both full transmission and the compact selected schedule; transmitted application bytes fell 68.4%. Compact payloads alone delivered most of the reduction. Search planning improved time over fixed sweeps, but incremental gains over previous RL were small. Extra MAPPO training increased crashes in an earlier trial. Adaptive age weighting and repeated confirmation scans have not shown a reliable gain. PyBullet validates simulated flight behavior only.

Independent fast-simulator controls:
* 160 new scenarios crossed with four channel conditions: silence found 152/160; reliable link with retries found 149/160; burst25 with retries found 152/160. Under burst25 estimated time saving versus silence was 0.43 s with paired 95% interval -0.43 to +1.43 s.
* Another 160 scenarios crossed with scattered/burst25 loss: two prompt attempts with no later retry and intervals of 8 or 16 produced identical per-mission outcomes and byte counts, 302/320, 14.78 s and 1,275 bytes on average. The period was not identified by training.
* Sensor and channel stress: on the same 160 new scenarios, with detection probability 0.40 and 50% blocked slots, send-once vs selected retries found 136 vs 133 targets for five-slot bursts and 137 vs 139 for one fifty-slot burst. With perfect links and detection probability 0.95 both found 154; under complete blackout and probability 0.40 both found 144. Comparisons within a condition are paired. Small count changes are exploratory; no transfer or superiority claim follows. Sensors model independent misses and no false positives.

## Decisive protocol and stopping rules

1. Freeze navigation, packet format, motion model and detector for each matched comparison. Train a small sequential transmit/wait policy using own scan novelty, age, remaining byte allowance, known sector boundary and public drift estimate. No hidden target state, link trace, acknowledgments, or undelivered teammate state. Include silence as an allowed action.
2. Compare silence, send once, send twice, staggered periodic, byte-matched randomized transmission, and the best simple rule chosen on validation. Enforce identical per-drone token budgets; report transmitted and delivered bytes and payload overhead assumptions. Compare silence separately since it spends zero. Include fixed-duration traffic measurements to isolate mission-length effects.
3. Separate independently generated training, validation and test scenarios. Freeze policy and acceptance margins before testing. Cross identical target trajectories and sensor draws with link traces; distinguish independent from shared blackouts. Resample missions as clusters and report variation across at least five independent training runs. Run a separate PyBullet transfer on a new mission set.
4. Proposed primary endpoint: mean capped time to first detection at a fixed byte ceiling with misses scored at the deadline. Define practical differences with the project supervisor before new tests; e.g., at least 1 simulated second saved, and a 95% lower bound on change in detection above -2 percentage points. These are proposed research margins; size the confirmatory experiment using paired pilot variance. Keep simulator sensitivity distinct from algorithm tuning.
5. Screen failure dimensions in order: frequent vs sparse scan opportunities; disjoint vs overlapping sectors; accurate vs biased drift; reliable vs weak or miscalibrated sensors; equal average loss in short and long bursts; common vs independent outages; low vs loose byte ceilings. Then check delayed, out-of-order delivery and header/contention costs if conclusions persist. Complete blackout and a perfect link are mandatory controls. Stress only plausible interactions and report all tested cells.
6. If competing policies choose the same messages under the tested cadence, stop attributing a gain to retry timing. If silence is competitive, report that communication has limited demonstrated value in that operating region. If a strong simple policy matches RL at equal cost or RL fails zero-shot, report the simpler rule and operating boundary. Increasing search overlap is a separately labeled scenario, never a replacement for reporting the original null result. Acknowledged links, obstacle-rich missions, false alarms, team-size changes and real-world performance remain outside the current evidence.
